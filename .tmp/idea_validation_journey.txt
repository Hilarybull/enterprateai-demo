Business Idea Validation User Journey v1.0 
Goal 
Help a pre-registration founder validate a business idea using structured inputs + 
deterministic modelling + scenario simulation, then output: 
• Validation Score (0–100) + reasons 
• Baseline business metrics 
• “What to change” recommendations 
• Saved assumptions for Blueprint (Module 2) 
 
Step 0: Entry Point 
User action: 
Clicks “Validate my Idea”. 
System action: 
Shows two options: 
1. Business Idea (no registered business) ✅ 
2. Product/Service Idea (existing business) 
User selects Business Idea.

Step 1: Setup Business Context 
UI: “Tell us what you’re building” 
Inputs 
• Business name (working name) 
• Business type: Service micro-business 
• Location (UK region/city) 
• Founder availability: hours/week 
• Stage: Idea 
System actions 
• Creates Business (draft) node 
• Creates Founder node 
• Sets defaults (currency GBP, UK assumptions) 
Example 
Cleaning: “SparklePro Cleaning”, London, 40 hrs/week 
 IT: “ByteBridge IT”, Manchester, 35 hrs/week 
 
Step 2: Problem + Target Customer 
UI: “Who is it for and what problem do you solve?” 
Inputs (structured, short) 
• Customer segment (dropdown + free text) 
• Problem type (cost / time / compliance / quality / reliability) 
• Frequency (daily/weekly/monthly) 
• Current alternatives used by customers (optional)


--- page 5 ---
System actions 
• Creates Idea node 
• Creates CustomerSegment node 
• Links: Idea → CustomerSegment 
Examples 
Cleaning 
• Customer: “Small offices (5–20 staff)” 
• Problem: “Inconsistent hygiene / unreliable cleaners” 
• Frequency: “3x weekly” 
IT 
• Customer: “SMEs 10–50 staff” 
• Problem: “No internal IT support” 
• Frequency: “Weekly incidents + ongoing maintenance” 
 
Step 3: Offer Definition 
UI: “Describe what you will sell” 
Inputs 
• Service type (e.g., cleaning, IT support, marketing) 
• Pricing model: hourly / fixed job / retainer 
• Price amount 
• Deliverable unit (hours / job / month) 
• Package tiers (optional: basic/standard/premium)


--- page 6 ---
System actions 
• Creates ProductService node(s) 
Examples 
Cleaning 
• Fixed job: £80 per office visit 
• Unit: 1 clean 
IT 
• Retainer: £2,500 per month 
• Unit: 1 client retainer 
 
Step 4: Demand Assumptions 
UI: “How many customers do you expect?” 
Inputs 
• Expected units per month (jobs/hours/retainers) 
• Expected number of customers 
• Sales cycle estimate (days) 
• Payment terms (7/14/30/60) 
System actions 
• Creates RevenueStream node 
• Stores assumption confidence = manual

Step 5: Cost Inputs (Variable + Fixed) 
UI: “What does it cost to deliver?” 
Inputs 
• Variable cost per unit (per job/hour/client) 
• Fixed monthly costs (insurance, tools, software, marketing) 
• Founder draw (optional) 
• Contractor costs (optional) 
System actions 
• Creates CostStructure entries (fixed/variable) 
• Links: CostStructure → ProductService/Business

Step 6: Capacity & Delivery Feasibility 
UI: “Can you actually deliver this volume?” 
Inputs 
• Founder hours/week 
• Staff/contractors count (initially 0–2) 
• Delivery capacity per unit (e.g., jobs per cleaner per day; hours per month) 
System actions 
• Creates Resource node(s) 
• Computes CapacityFeasibility


--- page 9 ---
Examples 
Cleaning 
• 1 cleaner can do 3 jobs/day 
• 20 workdays → 60 jobs/month per cleaner 
• Sarah has 1 cleaner initially → capacity 60 jobs 
• But demand assumption is 100 jobs → mismatch flagged 
IT 
• Founder capacity 80 hours/month 
• Service requires 30 hours/client/month 
• 3 clients → 90 hours → overload flagged 
 
Step 7: Optional - Cash Buffer (Enables runway) 
UI: “Do you have startup cash buffer?” 
Inputs 
• Cash buffer amount (GBP) 
• Optional: expected upfront costs 
System actions 
• Creates initial CashflowSnapshot 
• Enables runway and payment-delay stress tests 
Example 
Cleaning: £12,000 
 IT: £8,000


--- page 10 ---
Step 8: External Market Context (Auto Enrichment) 
UI: “Market context (UK benchmarks)” 
System actions (no user burden) 
• API Pulls: 
o wage benchmarks (for realism checks) 
o sector indicators (ONS category data) 
o optional Google demand trend index 
Rule: External context applies capped influence (±10%) and generates flags; does not 
overwrite the founder’s business model. 
Outputs shown to user 
• “Your wage assumption is below typical market range” (if applicable) 
• “Sector volatility suggests maintaining higher margin buffer” 
 
Step 9: Baseline Metrics Generated (Deterministic) 
UI: “Your Baseline Business Model” 
EnterprateAI calculates and shows: 
• Monthly revenue 
• Monthly variable cost 
• Monthly fixed cost 
• Net surplus/deficit 
• Contribution margin % 
• Break-even revenue 
• Capacity feasibility outcome 
• Optional runway (if cash buffer given)

• Dimension breakdown 
• Reason codes translated into plain English 
• “Top 3 improvements”

 Reasons: 
• Capacity mismatch (demand > delivery capacity)

Step 11: Simulation (Core: “Before you commit, 
simulate”) 
UI: “What are you planning next?” 
User runs preset scenarios: 
• Hire (add staff cost + capacity) 
• Revenue -20% 
• Costs +10% 
• Payment delay +14 days 
• Price +10% 
• Lose top client (if provided)
